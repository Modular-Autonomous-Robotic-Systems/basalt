# Take-off Bias Acquisition and Climb Divergence (2026-10-24)

This file records the analysis of the catastrophic divergence seen in every SITL run of 2026-10-24, made from the logs under `/ws/logs/slam` and from controlled replays of the lossless bag `/ws/data/telemetry_18`. It extends root causes R1, R2, R4, R5 and R8 of [`../plans/vio_drift_analysis.md`](../plans/vio_drift_analysis.md) with measurements on the current tree.

## 1. The failure mode

A diverging run reports a vertical climb that accelerates without bound while the true climb runs at a constant 3 to 4 m/s. In `2026-10-24_00-10-54` the estimated speed rises from 4.3 m/s at take-off plus 4 s to 69.5 m/s at plus 30 s, the estimated altitude reaches 510 m against 107 m, and the estimated path ends at 76.4 km against 811 m of ground truth. Triangulated landmark depth inflates with the velocity, from a median of 56 m at 5 to 10 s to 10.8 km at 30 to 45 s, so the world is rescaled consistently. The vision cost stays low throughout the climb (median reprojection error 0.3 to 0.7 px) and the inertial cost is small (3 to 20) against 200 to 470 in bounded runs, where the inertial term is seen resisting the vision term. The solver is therefore not failing to converge. It has found a self consistent wrong solution, a scaled world explained by a biased accelerometer, and the residuals only grow once the horizontal manoeuvre at about 45 to 60 s exposes the inconsistency. Association is not the origin, since `connected_ratio` stays at 0.3 to 0.5, comparable to bounded runs.

## 2. The predictor, the vertical accelerometer bias in the world frame

The outcome is predicted by the vertical component of `R_w_i * b_a`, computed from `vio_state.csv`, during the climb. Bounded runs hold it between minus 0.07 and minus 0.15 m/s^2 while their horizontal components reach 2.3 m/s^2, and diverging runs hold minus 0.37 to minus 2.32 m/s^2. The mechanism is geometric. In a straight vertical climb at constant velocity, vision fixes the direction of translation but not its magnitude, so an acceleration error along the direction of travel only rescales the trajectory and the landmark depths, which the images cannot refute. This is the constant velocity case of the Martinelli result in section 2.2.4 of the drift plan, specialised to the bias component parallel to the motion. Horizontal bias components are instead absorbed by the tilt confound of section 2.2.3, which is why a large horizontal bias is harmless here. Marginalisation then freezes whatever error accumulates (R5).

## 3. The bias is acquired in the first second of take-off, chaotically

Three replays of `telemetry_18` with identical configuration (`4c0e0bba`, `vio_zero_velocity_weight` 0) and identical binary differ only in a deterministic 10 percent frame drop applied by a relay. The vertical bias one second after take-off and the outcome were as follows.

| Run | Log directory | Frames | Vertical `b_a` at take-off plus 1 s | Speed ratio in the climb | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F, no drop | `2026-10-24_01-10-38` | 3424 | minus 0.03 | constant near 1.35, corrected to 1.0 to 1.1 by the manoeuvre | path 865 m, endpoint drift 13.0 m, SE3 ATE over 90 s 6.6 m |
| D, drop seed A | `2026-10-24_01-10-30` | 3072 | minus 0.17 to minus 0.22 | creeping from 1.25 to 2.25 | delayed divergence, path 55.2 km |
| E, drop seed B | `2026-10-24_01-10-34` | 3072 | minus 0.92 | 2 rising to 48 | divergence from the start, path 56.3 km |

The same data perturbed slightly gives all three regimes, so the take-off estimate is ill conditioned and its outcome is effectively random. Frame loss is neither the cause nor a protection. An earlier apparent correlation, that every lossy run before the 2026-10-23 shared memory fix stayed bounded and every lossless run after it diverged, was coincidental, as runs D and E diverged with loss and F stayed bounded without it. The live run `2026-10-24_01-11-31` with the same configuration acquired minus 0.09 to minus 0.41, over scaled the climb by about 2.5 and diverged at the horizontal manoeuvre, which is the delayed regime.

During take-off, between plus 3.5 and plus 5.5 s, interframe flow reaches 10 to 20 px mean and up to 31.6 px maximum, survival falls to 0.1 to 0.6, the median pre optimisation reprojection error rises to 1.2 to 11.5 px with a 95th percentile up to 21 px, and the first iteration vision cost reaches 1000 to 6200. The accelerometer bias moves within the same half second, for example in E from 0.09 to 0.84 m/s^2 between 4.0 and 4.5 s as the median reprojection error peaks at 9.8 px, and in `00-10-54` from 0.02 to 2.57 m/s^2 between 3.5 and 5.5 s. The odometry back end performs no outlier rejection (R8), so these residuals are absorbed by the weakest constrained state, the accelerometer bias, mostly along the direction of motion. The take-off flow against the 28 px capture range of three pyramid levels is R2. With `optical_flow_levels` 5 the capture range is larger, but survival on the ground fell to 0.2 to 0.33 in the 2026-10-24 configuration against 0.63 to 0.73 with three levels.

## 4. The phantom ground map when the parallax gate is off

With `vio_min_triangulation_parallax_deg` 0.0, the value in the current `data/sitl_config_vo.json`, the gate at `src/vi_estimator/sqrt_keypoint_vio.cpp:784-788` reduces to a cosine threshold of 1 and rejects nothing. Runs D, E and F then triangulate 16 to 40 landmarks before take-off at depths of 15 to 1239 m while the camera is within a metre of the ground, which is R1. With the gate at 0.9 degrees (`2026-10-20_22-31-02`, `2026-10-24_00-10-54`) no landmark exists before take-off and the map is built during the take-off transient instead. `00-10-54` still diverged, so the phantom map worsens the take-off but is not necessary for the failure.

## 5. The zero velocity constraint is inert

`mpIsStationary` (`src/vi_estimator/sqrt_keypoint_vio.cpp:485-490`) is false whenever all three of `vio_stationary_enable_shoe`, `vio_stationary_enable_mahalanobis` and `vio_stationary_enable_flow` are false, and the constraint applies only when `mpZeroVelocityWeight > 0 && mpIsStationary` (`:1655-1656`), which is the weight's only use. `is_stationary` in `vio_stationary.csv` is 0 on every frame of `2026-10-20_22-31-02`, `2026-10-24_00-10-54`, `00-51-47` and `01-11-31`, including the 4 to 5 s on the ground, and the first two ran with SHOE and Mahalanobis enabled. Changing `vio_zero_velocity_weight` between 100 and 0 therefore cannot change these runs, and any difference between two such runs is run to run variance of the take-off. With both detectors enabled, stage 1 and stage 2 are rarely true on the same frame, so the AND combination never fires even at rest.

## 5a. Run to run non-determinism, and the comparison with phase 1

The phase 1 flights (`phase_1_1`, `phase_1_2`, bags `telemetry_3` and the flight before it) flew the same mission as the 2026-10-24 flights, 4 s on the ground, a straight climb at about 4 m/s from 4 to 43 s, cruise at 150 m from 44 to 101 s, a hover at 150 m only from 101 to 107 s, then descent. They had no hover after take-off. Their calibration is identical to today's, and their configuration differs only in `optical_flow_levels` (3 then, 5 from `2026-10-24_00-38-59`) and `vio_init_accel_averaging` (absent then). Their take-off was as violent as today's (median reprojection error 5 to 20 px, `phase_1_2` first iteration vision cost 287205), and their vertical world frame accelerometer bias stayed between plus 0.10 and minus 0.32 m/s^2, consistent with the predictor of section 2.

Three replays with today's binary and the logged `phase_1_1` configuration, extended with the post 2026-10-03 keys at their inactive values (a missing key is fatal, see [`vio_config_json.md`](vio_config_json.md)), gave the following.

| Replay | Bag | Log directory | Path | Endpoint drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | `telemetry_3` | `2026-10-24_01-31-56` | 921 m | 17.0 m |
| H | `telemetry_3` | `2026-10-24_01-32-00` | 62.0 km | 59.7 km |
| I | `telemetry_16` | `2026-10-24_01-32-04` | 845 m | 4.8 m |

G and H had identical input, configuration and binary. Their `of_frame.csv` files are identical over all 3168 rows and their per frame inertial sample counts agree, yet the first substantive difference appears in the local mapper's `map_cull.csv` at plus 0.48 s (338 against 341 landmarks), because the local mapper runs on its own thread and the batch of keyframes it holds when it runs depends on scheduling. Its corrections are applied to the odometry states (`vio_pose_update.csv`, `applied` above zero from plus 0.4 s), the odometry state first differs at plus 1.75 s in the sixth decimal, the landmark sets differ by plus 2.1 s (31 against 37), and take-off amplifies the difference into a vertical bias of minus 0.06 against minus 0.37 m/s^2 at plus 6 s and into opposite outcomes. The estimator is therefore not deterministic on recorded data, and a single run of any configuration or code version cannot be compared with a single run of another. The coupling is the correction loop described in [`vio_localmapper_correction_loop.md`](vio_localmapper_correction_loop.md).

The old bags are still processed to a bounded result by today's binary in two of three runs, and today's bag `telemetry_18` in one of four replays across configurations, against nine bounded of ten runs before 2026-10-24 and one of ten after. No systematic change of code, configuration, simulator, camera, scene, heading, frame delivery or inertial timing was found to explain the shift. The simulator package dates from 2026-08-20 and `sitl_ws/config/airsim2` from 2026-08-22. A sharp, fully textured ground view (99 percent of 40 px cells with a corner against 35 to 45 percent) appears only in the first flight after the simulator starts (`telemetry_7`, `15`, `18`) and does not separate outcomes. Whether the rate shift is real needs several replays per bag with one configuration and one binary.

## 6. What is not logged

No log records keypoint image coordinates (`of_frame.csv`, `vio_landmarks.csv`, `map_tracks.csv` and `of_track_death.csv` carry counts and identifiers only), so the spatial distribution of landmarks in the image cannot be checked from the logs.

## 7. Method

Scripts used, kept outside the repository, compute per window statistics from `of_frame.csv`, `vio_reproj_pre_opt.csv`, `vio_state.csv`, `vio_triang.csv`, `vio_landmarks.csv`, `vio_assoc.csv`, `vio_solver_summary.csv` and the last and first iterations of `vio_solver_iter.csv`, plus Umeyama SE3 and Sim3 ATE on the rows of `gt_eval.csv` that carry ground truth. The frame drop relay subscribed to the bag's raw camera topic and republished it with `zlib.crc32("<seed>:<stamp>") % 100 < 10` frames removed, under the large shared memory profile, into Basalt's `camera-topic` in its own `ROS_DOMAIN_ID`.

## 8. Take-off excitation flights of 2026-10-25

Two SITL flights of the ArduPilot take-off excitation (`/ws/plans/arducopter_takeoff_behaviour.md`, 1 m take-off then interleaved ±5° roll and pitch lobes and a ±5° yaw pair while climbing at 1 m/s), `/ws/logs/slam/2026-10-25_20-20-15` and `2026-10-25_20-25-58`, with identical configuration (`vio_zero_velocity_weight` 0, all stationary detectors off, parallax gate 0.9°, three pyramid levels). The excitation was realised as designed, 4.55° tilt peaks and yaw peaks of 5.7 to 6.5°, but the outcome was decided before it began. In the first 0.5 s after take-off the flow reached 16 to 18 px mean and 20.6 px maximum, the map grew from 26 to 69 landmarks to about 200, the vertical world bias reached minus 0.9 to minus 1.65 m/s², and the speed ratio reached about 5. The 1 m take-off worsened the flow because the scene depth near the ground is about 1 m, and the flow scales as `f_x v / (Z f_c)`. During the excitation the vertical bias recovered to minus 0.08 (first run) and minus 0.4 to minus 0.6 m/s² (second run), as the observability analysis predicts, but the speed ratio did not. It rose to 34 in the first run, which diverged, and stayed at 6 to 7.5 in the second until the cruise. A bias corrected while the scale stays wrong shows the scale held by velocity and landmark depths in the marginalisation prior (R5). Every frame from take-off ended at the iteration limit (mean 8.0 iterations against `vio_max_iterations` 7, 12 to 15 percent rejected steps), and the horizontal world bias grew to 1.3 to 2.7 m/s². The conclusion is that excitation after take-off repairs the bias but not a scale fixed by the take-off transient, so the transient must be kept out of the estimator or tamed. The options are recorded in §5 of the excitation plan.

## 9. The slow take-off flight of 2026-10-25, and the ground phase as the first cause

`/ws/logs/slam/2026-10-25_20-54-58` flew the excitation mission with a leading `DO_CHANGE_SPEED` of 0.5 m/s, with configuration and calibration identical to §8. The slow take-off took effect (ground truth climb 0.57 to 0.59 m/s against 1.37 m/s before) but the run diverged worse, with the vertical world bias at minus 2.17 m/s² 0.6 s after take-off and a speed ratio of about 14. Mean flow at lift-off still reached 11.6 to 12.7 px, because at lift-off the camera is only tens of centimetres above the ground, so the 1 m scene depth assumed when sizing the slow take-off does not hold in the first 0.3 s.

The damage begins on the ground, before take-off, in this run and in run A of §8. With `vio_zero_velocity_weight` 0 and every stationary detector off, nothing holds the velocity at zero, and while the parallax gate keeps the window free of landmarks the estimator dead-reckons on the IMU alone. The estimated pose drifted 0.26 to 0.65 m over the 4 s on the ground in all three runs of 2026-10-25, at estimated speeds up to 0.15, 0.45 and 1.21 m/s. That drift fabricates a baseline, and the parallax gate, which tests the estimated geometry, accepts it. Run A triangulated 45 landmarks before take-off from 1.54 s before it, and this run 51 from 2.0 s before it, the first batch from an estimated baseline of 0.155 m at depths of 2 to 133 m while the vehicle stood still. Run B, which triangulated none on the ground, was the bounded one. Vision then contradicts the drifted motion, and the bias absorbs the conflict before the vehicle moves, 0.74 m/s² horizontal and a median reprojection error of 1.2 to 2.0 px at 1.3 to 1.0 s before take-off in this run. The landmark depths triangulated in the first 1.5 s after take-off had medians of 8.4 (A), 6.4 (B) and 17.0 m (this run), against a true depth of about 1 to 3 m, and those ratios match the speed ratios at take-off, so the scale error is set by the depths at which the first map is built. During the 2.5 s hover at 0.95 m after the take-off the estimate kept 2.5 to 4 m/s with a mean flow of 0.4 px, which landmarks at the inflated depths permit. The excitation then ran as designed and could not recover, and the vertical bias worsened from minus 0.4 to minus 2.4 m/s².

The conclusion is that the first cause is the unconstrained velocity on the ground, which the drift plan's phase 3a exists to remove, and that slowing the take-off treats a secondary symptom. Enabling one stationary detector with a non-zero `vio_zero_velocity_weight` is the direct test.

## 10. The flights of 2026-10-26, a clean ground phase and a tilt error

Three runs, `/ws/logs/slam/2026-10-26_22-26-49` (A, the excitation mission at 0.57 m/s), `2026-10-26_22-32-06` (B, a GUIDED take-off to 10 m without excitation) and `2026-10-26_22-42-26` (C, the excitation mission interrupted by GUIDED and restarted, so its take-off flew at 0.95 m/s and LAND cut the excitation in the first yaw lobe), share one configuration with SHOE and flow detectors, the start latch at 3 frames and `vio_zero_velocity_weight = 100`. All three are in `/ws/logs/00000057.BIN`, whose clock equals the Basalt stamps. The ground phase is now clean, with 93 to 98.5 percent of ground frames stationary, no stationary frame after motion onset, 1.6 to 3.6 cm of drift on the ground and no landmark triangulated before lift-off, so the first cause of §9 is removed.

The bias that settles high after lift-off is a tilt error. The angle between Basalt's gravity direction in the IMU frame and the ground truth one reached 9.8° in B and 13.1° in C one second after onset and 17.7° in C at 10 s, while the body bias was almost purely horizontal, `(2.15, 1.97, −0.15)` m/s² in C, and `g sin 17.2° = 2.90 m/s²` matches its magnitude. The true accelerometer bias is near zero, since `SIM_ACC1_BIAS` is zero and ArduPilot adds no bias process. In C the first landmark came 0.42 s after lift-off at 16 px of mean flow, the first batch had plausible depths of 0.36 to 1.10 m, later batches inflated to 5.8 m mean at 1.2 m altitude, every frame hit the iteration cap of 8, and the tilt error jumped by about 5° in single frames at 4.49 s and 5.59 s, solver steps along the confound rather than gyroscope integration. In A the slowed climb kept the tilt below 1.6° but collapsed the scale (0.05 m/s estimated against 0.37 m/s true), and the tilt broke in the 2.45 s hover dwell before `NAV_SCRIPT_TIME` started, jumping from 0.6° to 6.5° and rising to 12.1° at 0.3 to 0.8 px of flow with 4 to 6 of 8 solver steps rejected. The excitation then did not repair the tilt in either run.

The excitation drifted the real vehicle 1.6 m horizontally in A and in C, because the EKF attitude was 0.1 to 0.3° off the simulator truth and the guided angle controller has no position feedback. `PSCN` and `PSCE` freeze under angle control and cannot measure this, so `SIM` attitude and the Basalt ground truth were used. The climb is also no longer vertical because the 1 m take-off leaves the climb to 150 m to the slanted leg to the first waypoint. Two settings make the confound cheap, `accel_noise_std` at 6.8 times the simulator density in the working tree (`imu_noise_parameters.md` §7.6) and an accelerometer bias prior of standard deviation 0.1 m/s², which reaches the accelerometer through `vio_init_bg_weight` because of the swap in `vio_residuals_and_priors.md`. The second, position controlled excitation and the SITL sensor model proposed in response are in `/ws/plans/arducopter_takeoff_behaviour.md` §6 and §7.


## Tilt and horizontal accelerometer bias are traded one for one (added 2026-11-06)

Replays of `/ws/data/telemetry_40` and live `sitl2` runs on 2026-11-06 show, in every configuration, that the horizontal accelerometer bias magnitude equals $|\mathbf{g}| \sin(\text{tilt error})$ to within about 0.1 m/s², with the bias vector opposite to the body frame error of the gravity direction. A 10° tilt error appears as about 1.7 m/s² of bias, 27° as 4.4 m/s². At constant attitude the accelerometer cannot separate the two, so the tilt is anchored only by the absolute accelerometer bias prior, which is `vio_init_ba_weight` on the first state ($\sigma \approx 0.32$ m/s² at the default 10) carried through the marginalised chain. The bias random walk penalises only differences between consecutive biases and does not anchor a uniform shift. Because the SITL IMU is ideal (`GenerateNoise: false`), any estimated accelerometer bias in SITL is tilt error, and the growth of the $x$ bias seen in live runs is this trade. The diagnostic is cheap, compare $|\mathbf{b}_{a,xy}|$ in `vio_state.csv` with $|\mathbf{g}| \sin$ of the tilt error computed from `gt_eval.csv` as the angle between $\mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{e}_z$ of the estimate and of ground truth, which does not depend on yaw. Raising `vio_init_ba_weight` to $10^4$ cut the take off tilt error from 2.4° to 6° down to 1.5° to 3.5° with the gravity and scale mode off, but the bias still reached 0.7 m/s², so the anchor leaks somewhere, possibly through local mapper pose updates that rebuild frozen keyframe linearisation points (`src/vi_estimator/sqrt_keypoint_vio.cpp:640-650`). The interaction with the gravity and scale mode is recorded in `plans/gravity_scale_optimisation.md` §4.3.

Two pitfalls of running these experiments. The logger names its directory by the wall clock second, so two replays started in the same second write interleaved, corrupted files into one directory, as `/ws/logs/slam/2026-11-06_17-16-53` shows. Start parallel replays at least a second apart. A live `sitl2` stack on ROS domain 0 contaminates a replay on the same domain (`/ws/context/djinn-workflows.md`, `djinn start basalt-replay`).
